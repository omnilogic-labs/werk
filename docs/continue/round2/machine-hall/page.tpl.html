werk Plant & Capacity

Internal pitch · September 2026

Lights-out working

The plant
doesn't stop
when you go home.

werk starts a process somewhere and lets you come back to it later. Locally, on a machine you can ssh to, or in a container it provisions. It puts your repository there on a fresh branch, gives you a terminal that survives your laptop closing, and shows you every one of those — across every machine — in one list.

The workload that matters

Coding agents are command-line programs. Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Aider. werk dispatches and monitors them with no per-agent integration and no plugin for each one — so an agent released next month works on the day you install it.

01
Dispatch & return
In service — working today

Start it. Shut the lid. Come back.

You are at your own terminal. You name the work and say where it should run — the Mac mini on the desk, a VPS, a container werk provisions, the company Kubernetes cluster. werk gets there, puts your repository down on a fresh branch, and starts the agent in a terminal on that machine.

Then you close the laptop. The session is not on your laptop. Nothing is holding it up; nothing needs the lid open, the VPN on, or the ssh connection alive. The machine runs whether or not you are in the room, and that is the whole point of building a plant instead of doing the work by hand.

At the kitchen table, hours later, you type werk. There is the fleet, one line each. werk attach parser-rewrite and you are inside the session, scrollback intact, mid-conversation with an agent that has been working the entire time. When it is done the commits are sitting on the branch, ready to be pulled like castings off a line.

Instrumentation

No bespoke gauge for every machine.

A plant does not fit a custom sensor to each press. It reads the standard signals every machine on the floor already emits. werk does the same thing with terminal programs, which is why it needs to know nothing about any particular agent.

BELThe bell. Something is asking for you — the oldest attention signal there is, and agents still ring it.
OSC 9 · OSC 777Desktop notifications. Whatever the program wanted to tell you, werk puts on the list instead.
OSC 9;4Progress. The percentage in the register is the program's own, not a guess.
OSC 133Command boundaries and exit status. Where each command started, stopped, and whether it worked.

The two universal interfaces to a repository, for people and for agents alike, are the terminal and git. werk is about making both trivially easy to reach — and everything else follows from refusing to build a per-agent adapter for a category of tool that is being reinvented every few weeks.

02
Two on one panel
On the drawing board — designed, not yet built

Hand someone the panel while it is still running.

The oldest thing in heavy industry is the handover: the person who knows the machine standing at the console next to the person taking it on, both looking at the same gauges, one of them with a hand on the controls.

This is what werk is being designed to do with a session. You have an agent halfway through something knotted and you want a second pair of eyes on it. You send a colleague a live link to that terminal. They open it in a browser and they are in — the same scrollback, the same running process, watching the output arrive as you do. Either of you can type.

No screen share, no recording, no reconstructing what happened from a paste. The session itself is the thing you hand over, and it carries on running while you both stand at it.

This is planned work. It does not exist yet. What exists today is werk serve in a browser on your own network.

What the link is meant to carry

Same terminal
Not a video of it. The live session, with its scrollback.
Both hands
Either person can type into it.
Nothing installed
They open a browser. They do not need werk, the repository, or an account on the machine.
Still running
The agent does not pause because two people are watching.
03
The mimic board
On the drawing board — designed, not yet built

The whole works on one wall.

Every control room has one: a schematic of the entire plant with a lamp for each vessel, so one person standing in front of it can see what the works is doing without walking a metre of it.

The company-wide view is that board for agent sessions. Every session in the organisation on one screen — each person, the sessions they are running, and the subagents those sessions have spawned, nested under them. Lamps for who is working, who is blocked, who has finished and left commits behind.

And it is not a dashboard you only read. Any tile on it opens onto its live terminal, exactly as if you had attached from your own machine. Someone's overnight run has gone sideways at 06:00 and you can be inside it before they are awake.

This is planned work. The panel below is the design, not a screenshot.

Capacity you can
see is capacity
you will use.

A machine you cannot see the state of is a machine you do not trust, and a machine you do not trust runs at a fraction of what it could. The register, the shared session and the mimic board are all the same argument: put the whole floor in front of the people who own it, and the floor gets worked.

Plant status

Commissioned, and on the board.

In service
  • Dispatching a session to your own machine, anything you can ssh to, a container werk provisions, or a Kubernetes cluster.
  • The repository put down on a fresh branch, and a terminal that survives the laptop closing.
